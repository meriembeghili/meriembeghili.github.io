About

I am a philosopher working at the intersection of ethics, social robotics, and healthcare technologies.

I am completing a PhD in philosophy at Sorbonne Université (ISIR–CNRS, expected defense July 2026), on the ethical issues raised by the use of social robots in healthcare. Since 2026 I have also been a Research Engineer at ISIR (CNRS), on the INNOVCARE project — an interdisciplinary effort on care-led innovation for elderly autonomy.

My work moves between conceptual analysis and the field: qualitative interviews with healthcare professionals and roboticists, ethical risk assessment, and close collaboration with engineers on human–robot interaction in care settings.

Research

Thesis. Ethical Issues in the Use of Social Robots in Healthcare — supervised by Anouk Barberousse and Mohamed Chetouani. Affiliations: Sciences, Normes, Décision (SND), Institut des Systèmes Intelligents et de Robotique (ISIR–CNRS), Sorbonne Center for Artificial Intelligence (SCAI).

My research asks what is at stake — ethically, relationally, and institutionally — when robots enter care relationships: autonomy and its natural/artificial divide, trust in human–robot interaction, the invisible labour of care personnel, and the conditions for morally accountable design.

I contribute to interdisciplinary projects including INNOVCARE (France 2030 / ANR, France–Japan) and TAPAS (Franco-Japanese collaboration).
